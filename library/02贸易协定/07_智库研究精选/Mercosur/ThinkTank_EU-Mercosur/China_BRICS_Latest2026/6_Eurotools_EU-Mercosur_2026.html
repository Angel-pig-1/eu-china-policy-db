Mercosur–EU Agreement in 2026

The EU has reached one of the most ambitious breakthroughs in its modern history. The Mercosur Agreement positions Europe at the heart of one of the world’s largest trade zones.

Together, the EU and Mercosur form a market of around 780 million people and create the largest EU trade deal ever concluded with Latin America (Brazil, Argentina, Uruguay, and Paraguay). Although politically agreed, the treaty still awaits ratification, with reservations coming mainly from France, Ireland, Austria, and Poland. Yet momentum continues to build as 2026 approaches.

The core of the deal is simple: lower barriers, fewer tariffs, wider opportunity. The Agreement does reduction of tariffs on key trade flows:

  • From Mercosur → EU
  • Beef and meat products
  • Sugar and soy
  • Bioethanol
  • Aluminium
  • Other agricultural goods

From EU → Mercosur

  • Automobiles and commercial vehicles
  • Industrial machinery
  • Chemical and pharmaceutical products
  • Technology and engineering goods
  • European companies also gain:
  • Easier access to public procurement contracts
  • Stronger protection for regional brands and origin labels (Champagne, Parma, Roquefort and others)
  • More secure investment and intellectual property conditions

Here are the reasons, why Europe Supports the Agreement:

1. A Major Boost for European Industry

Under the agreement, most of these duties drop to near 0%. This instantly improves the competitiveness of this:

  • German machinery
  • Italian manufacturing lines
  • Slovak and Czech components
  • Industrial tools and spare parts supplied by companies like Eurotools

For Eurotools, this means:
exporting knives, screens, spare parts, and wear elements suddenly becomes up to 35% more price-competitive — opening doors that were previously too costly to enter.

 

2. Strategic Independence From China

Global supply chains are tightening. Europe aims to diversify away from overdependence on:

  • China
  • Russia
  • The Middle East

Mercosur countries offer what the EU needs:

  • Metals and mineral ores
  • Lithium and energy reserves
  • Agricultural raw materials
  • Large-scale industry

Latín America is also politically closer to Europe — a stable direction in uncertain times.

 

3. A Market Seeking Technology

Brazil and Argentina are industrial powerhouses in agriculture, mining, biomass, and waste processing. But equipment, recycling capacity, and automation lag behind European standards. That gap is opportunity. Growing demand is forecast for:

  • Shredders
  • Crushers and separators
  • Recycling screens
  • Industrial cutting knives
  • Wear parts for processing plants and waste systems

Eurotools fits precisely into this need — providing ready-to-ship components and OEM-compatible parts directly from Europe. 

Slovakia — a highly industrial export economy — stands to benefit enormously. Firms supplying tools, components, technology, and industrial services will see more open doors, more competitive pricing, and new markets that were once out of reach. While the political process is not finished, the message is clear: Europe is choosing cooperation over isolation, global engagement over shrinking borders. In a period marked by rising protectionism, this agreement keeps markets moving forward.

For companies like Eurotools — focused on delivering knives, screens, and industrial spare parts ready for immediate shipment — Latin America represents but a practical path to long-term growth.
