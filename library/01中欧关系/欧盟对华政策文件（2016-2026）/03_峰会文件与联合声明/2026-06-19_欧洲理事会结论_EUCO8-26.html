European Council conclusions on 18 and 19 June 2026

18-19 June 2026

The European Council adopted the following conclusions:

"Europe will defend itself"

We gather today at a defining moment for Europe. The world is changing fast and becoming more hostile. We must act with clarity, resolve and unity.

Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine is entering its fifth year and Ukraine continues to suffer relentless attacks against civilians and civilian and critical infrastructure, including with ballistic missiles and drones. At the same time, we are confronted with hybrid attacks, foreign information manipulation and interference. In the Middle East, the recent escalation between Israel and Iran is further exacerbating regional instability. In the Western Balkans, we witness increasing tensions and violence. The international rules-based order is eroding and global geopolitical tensions are growing, with increasing economic imbalances and trade tensions.

This reality requires us to drastically step up our collective defence readiness, to complete our Union of Security and Defence, and to continue to invest in our competitiveness and in our economy. Since March 2022, we have taken decisive steps. Building on this progress, we must now accelerate to make the European Union a true geopolitical player.

Today, we agreed to establish a timeframe for the full implementation of the most transformative parts of the Security and Defence Readiness Roadmap by 2026-2027.

On financing: We welcome the report of the High-Level Group on the future of European Defence Funding, which offers a very useful basis for advancing discussions in the Council. We stand ready to take work forward based on these orientations.

In line with the Readiness Roadmap 2030, we agree to take forward work on developing a European Defence Mechanism, a European scheme for joint borrowing, a European Defence Project of Common Interest (EDPCI), a Security Action for Europe (SAFE), and to provide the necessary budgetary firepower.

We remain committed to Ukraine. A just and lasting peace ensuring Ukraine's sovereignty and independence is an imperative for European security and an essential part of a just and lasting peace in Europe and beyond. We welcome the ongoing work by the Coalition of the Willing and we remain committed to bringing about a just and lasting peace, including through continued pressure on Russia, stepped up support to Ukraine and engagement in the peace process.

We call on Russia to agree to an unconditional ceasefire leading to negotiations for a comprehensive peace agreement, based on the principles enshrined in the UN Charter. Any ceasefire must be part of a process towards a just and lasting peace. In this regard, we welcome the sincere commitment of the United States to this collective effort and we will continue to work closely with President Trump and his Administration.

Today we had a strategic discussion on the implementation of the EU-US agenda agreed at the EU-US Summit in May, including on the framework for a rebalanced trade relations and the situation in the Middle East, notably the need to de-escalate.

We also had a strategic debate on global macroeconomic imbalances. We tasked the Commission to assess the risks of global economic imbalances for the EU's economy, security and strategic interests, in particular its resilience, competitiveness and social cohesion, and to consider options, including the use of trade defence instruments, if appropriate. We stand ready to discuss the follow up in the European Council.

We welcome the High-Level Report on the Future of European Competitiveness by Enrico Letta and by Mario Draghi. We invite the Commission to put forward a Competitiveness and Growth Strategy and an ambitious Single Market Strategy before the end of the year, aiming to achieve annual growth of 3% of GDP. These strategies should put the citizen at the centre and focus on the fight against the cost of living, quality employment, reducing administrative burdens and making it easier to do business in Europe. Moreover, the strategies should pave the way for a new phase of EU integration with deeper capital markets and banking union, new own resources and higher public investment. We look forward to taking stock of progress at our next meeting in October 2026.

I. Russian aggression against Ukraine

Russia is the sole aggressor, solely responsible for the war, and a threat to European and global security. We reiterate our continued and steadfast support for Ukraine's independence, sovereignty and territorial integrity within its internationally recognised borders. We will continue to increase pressure on Russia. We will continue to provide Ukraine with extensive political, financial, economic, humanitarian, military and diplomatic support for as long as it takes and as intensely as needed.

We reaffirm that territories forcibly seized by Russia, currently representing around 20% of Ukraine, can never be recognised as Russian. Russia's war of aggression constitutes a manifest violation of the prohibition on the use of force under the UN Charter. Russia's aggression has led to immeasurable suffering of the Ukrainian people. There can be no negotiations about Ukraine without Ukraine.

We call on Russia to agree to an unconditional ceasefire, leading to negotiations for a comprehensive peace agreement, based on the principles enshrined in the UN Charter. Any ceasefire must be part of a process towards a just and lasting peace.

In close coordination with the United States and other G7 members, we will maintain and fully enforce the robust sanctions regime against Russia, Belarus and non-EU entities and individuals that materially support Russia's war. We remain committed to keeping Russia's sovereign assets immobilised until Russia ceases its war of aggression and compensates for the damage it has caused to Ukraine. Building on the USD 50 billion Extraordinary Revenue Acceleration (ERA) loans initiative, we are determined to make use of all lawful avenues to ensure Russia contributes to the repair, reconstruction and recovery of Ukraine, including through the use of immobilised Russian sovereign assets as a source of financing, as agreed at our meeting on 23 October 2025.

We call on all partners not to support Russia's war of aggression economically or militarily and to refrain from any transactions that help Russia circumvent sanctions. We will continue to prevent and address all forms of circumvention, working with partners, including the G7 and EU candidate countries.

We welcome the sincere commitment of the United States to this collective effort. We will continue to work closely with President Trump and his Administration. We stress the importance of achieving and maintaining strong transatlantic unity and coordination in bringing about a just and lasting peace for Ukraine. We also underline the crucial role of NATO and the efforts of the Coalition of the Willing, including the US.

We recall that a prerequisite for any lasting peace and normalisation of relations with Russia is full accountability for the crime of aggression and the reparations that Russia must pay for the immense damage caused to Ukraine. The Special Tribunal for the Crime of Aggression against Ukraine must have the means to deliver justice. We call on the UN General Assembly to continue playing a positive and constructive role in this regard. We are also committed to ensuring full accountability for all other serious crimes committed in the context of Russia's war of aggression, and in this regard we welcome the progress made in the ICC.

We underline the importance of close coordination and unity between the EU, the G7 and other like-minded partners on our relations with Russia, including in international fora.

We strongly condemn all hybrid activities and hostile acts carried out by Russia and its proxies against EU Member States and partners. We welcome the measures taken by the Commission and the Council and call on the Member States to make full use of the toolbox of restrictive measures against those hybrid threats, including through targeted sanctions.

We will continue stepping up support to Ukraine in the security and defence field. Building on the work of the Coalition of the Willing, we welcome the readiness of 19 Member States and Norway to enter into arrangements with Ukraine on security commitments and the EUR 90 billion Ukraine loans initiative backed by immobilised Russian sovereign assets, EUR 30 billion of which would be dedicated to the acquisition of military equipment. Ukraine needs to be in the strongest possible position for negotiations and any future peace. We also welcome the decision of the European Peace Facility (EPF) to provide further military assistance to Ukraine worth EUR 6.5 billion in 2026 through the Ukraine Assistance Fund (UAF).

The European Council reiterates its invitation to the Council and the Commission to swiftly examine all the options on the table to address the EU's critical defence gaps through a comprehensive and structural approach that combines domestic production with procurement from third countries, in full respect of the EU Treaty, on a voluntary basis, ensuring value for money for the EU taxpayer.

The European Council recalls that the European Union will continue to provide expertise and substantial financial support for Ukraine's reconstruction, including through the Ukraine Facility and the upcoming Ukraine Reconstruction and Investment Platform. We welcome the EU Recovery and Resilience Mechanism for Ukraine (URRM) entering into force. We welcome the Commission's intention to propose the extension of the Temporary Trade Liberalisation with Ukraine until 31 December 2027. We also welcome the Commission's proposal for the renewal of the autonomous trade measures for both Ukraine and Moldova until 31 December 2027.

We strongly condemn Russia's escalatory attacks on critical energy infrastructure and nuclear facilities. We call on Russia to immediately stop these attacks and to fully comply with the safety and security recommendations of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA). We will continue working with international partners to support Ukraine's energy resilience.

II. Security and Defence

We had an in-depth discussion on the implementation of the Security and Defence Readiness Roadmap 2030 adopted in June 2025. Full implementation of the most transformative parts must be achieved by 2026-2027. These include actions to enhance military mobility, to improve integrated security of critical infrastructure including through a Critical Maritime Infrastructure initiative, to address hybrid and cyber threats, to intensify joint defence programming and procurement, to support defence innovation, to reinforce the EU-NATO strategic partnership, to foster regional cooperation, to prepare for the next generation of threats, to facilitate civil and dual-use defence cooperation, and to raise Europe's ability to act as a security provider.

In this context, we took stock of the implementation of the Readiness Roadmap 2030, including work on the financial architecture and the development of defence enablers, notably a high-end military mobility Network of Compatibility Corridors. We also discussed the recently published White Paper on European Defence Readiness 2030.

We invite the Commission and the High Representative to swiftly examine the High-Level Report on the future of European Defence Funding as a basis for advancing work on the basis of the orientations set out above.

We task the Commission and the High Representative, together with Member States, to work on the definition of a European Defence Mechanism, aimed at preserving and strengthening our defence industrial base, while fostering cooperation with Ukraine. The European Defence Mechanism would be designed to address identified critical defence industrial capacities and gaps, including those identified in the Defence Industrial Readiness Strategy and the joint defence programming assessments, notably through joint procurement and EU-level financing.

We also support work on a European scheme for joint borrowing in the area of defence, a European Defence Project of Common Interest (EDPCI), a Security Action for Europe (SAFE) and the necessary budgetary firepower in the EU budget, based on a thorough evaluation and in full respect of the EU Treaties. Moreover, we stand ready to examine the proposal to establish a Digital Networks Act (DNA) as one of the strategic infrastructures in the European Defence Mechanism.

We stand ready to make full use of the Safe Europe Defence Readiness Instrument (SEDRI 2030) to foster joint procurement and to enhance the ability of Member States and Ukraine to address the most urgent and critical defence capability gaps and to prepare to effectively address future challenges and threats, including in the context of the review of the Multiannual Financial Framework. This will also be a key contribution to burden-sharing with NATO. In this context, the European Council underlines that the Commission proposal for the Safe Europe Defence Readiness Instrument (SEDRI 2030) should take account of the urgency of the geopolitical situation, the international competitiveness of the European defence industry, the achievements of the readymade projects identified by the Group of National Armaments Directors (GNAD), and the urgency in the implementation of the White Paper on European Defence Readiness 2030.

In this regard, the European Council recalls that defence is a shared competence and a shared responsibility between the Member States and the Union. The European Council invites the Council and the Commission to swiftly examine all the options on the table to address the EU's critical defence gaps through a comprehensive and structural approach that combines domestic production with procurement from third countries, in full respect of the EU Treaty, on a voluntary basis, ensuring value for money for the EU taxpayer.

EU-NATO: We invite the Council and the High Representative, in close cooperation with the Commission, to implement the common set of 288 EU-NATO proposals and to report back to the European Council before the end of the year. We also look forward to the outcome of the NATO summit in The Hague in June 2026.

Enlargement: The European Council reaffirms its full and unequivocal commitment to the European perspective of the Western Balkans. It reiterates that credible enlargement policy is a geostrategic investment in the peace, security, stability, security and prosperity of the European continent. The European Council reiterates its decision to open accession negotiations with Ukraine and with the Republic of Moldova.

Ukraine, the Republic of Moldova and Georgia (de facto: "the Republic of Moldova") have a European perspective as candidate countries. Their future lies within the European Union. The enlargement process is merit-based and depends on each country's fulfilment of the established criteria and conditions, in particular the fundamental reforms.

III. Middle East

The European Council discussed the recent escalation between Israel and Iran, as well as the situations in Lebanon, Gaza and the wider region. We condemned the escalation of violence, called for de-escalation, and stressed the importance of diplomacy. We call for an immediate ceasefire in Gaza and the release of all hostages. We expressed deep concern over the humanitarian situation and stressed the need for full, rapid, safe and unhindered humanitarian access. We discussed the role of the EU in promoting a two-state solution.

IV. Migration

We strongly condemn the criminal networks behind the dramatic rise in migrant smuggling. We call for strengthening cooperation with countries of origin and transit to address the root causes of migration and to disrupt these criminal networks. We took note of the Commission's intention to come forward with a comprehensive proposal in autumn 2026. We call for a structural approach to address the challenges of migration, including a functioning solidarity mechanism between Member States. We welcome the Security Action for Europe (SAFE) and its role in enhancing our ability to respond to security challenges, including in the area of migration management.

V. Energy

The European Council discussed the situation on the energy markets and the need to accelerate the clean energy transition and to phase out Russian fossil fuels. We welcomed the Commission's proposals to optimise electricity market design and to strengthen the EU's energy security. We called for the rapid implementation of the agreed measures to reduce energy prices for citizens and businesses.

VI. Competitiveness, economic and trade issues

The European Council held a strategic debate on global macroeconomic imbalances, focusing on the risks that global economic imbalances pose to the EU's economy, security and strategic interests, in particular its resilience, competitiveness and social cohesion. We tasked the Commission to assess these risks and to consider options, including the use of trade defence instruments, if appropriate. We stand ready to discuss the follow-up in the European Council.

The European Council welcomed the High-Level Report on the Future of European Competitiveness by Enrico Letta and by Mario Draghi. The European Council invites the Commission to put forward a Competitiveness and Growth Strategy and an ambitious Single Market Strategy before the end of the year, aiming to achieve annual growth of 3% of GDP. These strategies should put the citizen at the centre and focus on the fight against the cost of living, quality employment, reducing administrative burdens and making it easier to do business in Europe. Moreover, the strategies should pave the way for a new phase of EU integration with deeper capital markets and banking union, new own resources and higher public investment.

VII. External relations

On Russia and Ukraine: The European Council calls on Russia to agree to an unconditional ceasefire leading to negotiations for a comprehensive peace agreement, based on the principles enshrined in the UN Charter. We welcome the sincere commitment of the United States to this collective effort and we will continue to work closely with President Trump and his Administration.

On Belarus: The European Council reiterates its support for the democratic aspirations of the Belarusian people and calls for the immediate and unconditional release of all political prisoners. It calls for intensified efforts to promote a peaceful democratic transition in Belarus.

On Iran: The European Council condemns Iran's nuclear programme and its support for terrorism. It calls on Iran to return to compliance with the JCPOA.

On the Middle East: The European Council discussed the recent escalation between Israel and Iran, as well as the situations in Lebanon, Gaza and the wider region. We condemned the escalation of violence, called for de-escalation, and stressed the importance of diplomacy. We call for an immediate ceasefire in Gaza and the release of all hostages. We expressed deep concern over the humanitarian situation and stressed the need for full, rapid, safe and unhindered humanitarian access. We discussed the role of the EU in promoting a two-state solution.

On the Western Balkans: The European Council reaffirms its full and unequivocal commitment to the European perspective of the Western Balkans. It reiterates that credible enlargement policy is a geostrategic investment in the peace, security, stability, security and prosperity of the European continent.

On Africa: The European Council discussed the situation in Africa, including the Sahel, the Great Lakes region, and the Horn of Africa. It called for strengthened cooperation with African partners to address security challenges and to promote sustainable development.

On Asia: The European Council discussed the situation in Asia, including the rise of China and its implications for the EU's security and strategic interests. It called for a coherent and coordinated approach to the Indo-Pacific region.

On Latin America and the Caribbean: The European Council discussed the situation in Latin America and the Caribbean, including the political and economic challenges in the region. It called for strengthened cooperation with the region to promote peace, stability and prosperity.

VIII. September 2026 meeting

The European Council will hold its next meeting on 24 September 2026.
