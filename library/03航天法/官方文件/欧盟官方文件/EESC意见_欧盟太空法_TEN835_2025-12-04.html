OPINION of the European Economic and Social Committee on the 'Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on the safety, resilience and sustainability of space activities in the Union'

A. Need for an EU space law

（前言部分：欧经社认为欧盟太空法的出台具有必要性，与EUSPA、ESA及各成员国国家太空法互补……）

General comments

1.1. The EESC supports the objective of establishing a harmonised, predictable and innovation‑friendly legal framework for space activities within the Union, aligned with the Union's strategic objectives on security, competitiveness, sustainability and technological sovereignty.

1.2. At the same time, the EESC considers that the proposal as it stands presents elements of complexity, fragmentation and insufficient clarity in the division of institutional responsibilities between the Commission, the European Union Agency for the Space Programme (EUSPA) and the Member States, and that these aspects should be significantly streamlined.

1.3. The EESC underlines that the establishment of a Union‑level authorisation and supervision framework, while positive in principle, risks overlapping with existing national frameworks and duplicating procedures for operators, unless accompanied by genuine simplification.

1.4. The EESC highlights that the proposal must recognise the strategic role of the European Space Agency (ESA) and of existing industrial and scientific ecosystems, and must promote complementarity with national initiatives and programmes, avoiding any impression that the new framework is intended to supplant them.

1.5. The EESC stresses the importance of ensuring that small and medium‑sized enterprises (SMEs), start‑ups, research entities and public‑private consortia are not subject to disproportionate administrative or financial burdens, and that a genuine "one‑stop shop" is created for authorisations.

1.6. The EESC also stresses the importance of providing for reciprocal access for third‑country entities to the Union's space market and services, in compliance with the principle of reciprocity and with the Union's international obligations, with a view to avoiding unjustified discrimination and guaranteeing the competitiveness of the European space sector.

1.7. The EESC points out that the introduction of the Union Registry of Space Objects (URSO), while representing an important step towards the transparent and safe management of space traffic, must be accompanied by clear mechanisms for the interoperability of existing national and international registers, with particular reference to the United Nations Register of Objects Launched into Outer Space.

1.8. The EESC stresses that the proposal should include a clear definition of "space activities" and "space services" in line with international law and with the definitions adopted by the United Nations Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (UNCOPUOS), in order to avoid legal uncertainty and regulatory fragmentation.

1.9. The EESC also considers it essential to ensure consistency with other Union legal instruments in the field of security, defence, telecommunications, environmental protection and competition, in order to avoid overlaps, inconsistencies or gaps in the European regulatory framework.

1.10. The EESC notes with concern that the proposal does not yet provide a clear and predictable framework for liability and insurance for damage caused by space activities, nor does it provide for a clear mechanism for the settlement of disputes, and invites the co‑legislators to introduce specific provisions to this end.

1.11. The EESC stresses that the new Union framework should be accompanied by a gradual and proportionate approach to implementation, with a sufficient transitional period for operators and national administrations, and with specific support measures for SMEs and emerging industrial actors.

1.12. The EESC underlines the importance of integrating environmental sustainability criteria throughout the entire space activity life cycle, consistent with the Union's climate objectives and with international best practice on space debris mitigation.

1.13. The EESC highlights the need to ensure effective protection of critical space infrastructure against cyber and hybrid threats, promoting coordination with the relevant Union and national authorities and consistent application of existing cybersecurity requirements.

1.14. The EESC considers that a permanent, inclusive and transparent multi‑level governance framework should be established, involving all relevant institutional, industrial, scientific and social stakeholders, in order to ensure that the new regulatory framework for space activities is effective, up‑to‑date and perceived as legitimate by all actors concerned.

1.15. The EESC calls on the Commission to ensure that the proposal is subject to a rigorous and transparent impact assessment concerning the administrative, economic and social consequences, in particular for SMEs and for emerging industrial actors, and that it is accompanied by a specific communication strategy.

1.16. The EESC lastly stresses the need to strengthen the European dimension of training, research and innovation in the space sector, promoting cross‑border cooperation and the dissemination of knowledge in order to ensure a high level of expertise and competitiveness for the European space sector.

Simplification and administrative burden

2.1. The EESC notes that one of the declared objectives of the proposal is to simplify and streamline administrative procedures for space activities, but considers that the text as presented does not yet achieve this objective.

2.2. The EESC stresses that the new authorisation framework should constitute a genuine simplification for operators, avoiding multiple or overlapping authorisations and different national procedures, and that it should be based on the principle of mutual recognition of national authorisations.

2.3. The EESC considers that authorisation requirements should be proportionate to the scale and nature of the activities and to the level of risk, and that a differentiated approach should be provided for SMEs, start‑ups, universities and research centres, in order to avoid discouraging innovation and participation in the European space programme.

2.4. The EESC highlights that the proposal should introduce a "one‑stop shop" mechanism for authorisations, with a single national contact point responsible for coordinating all stages of the procedure, and that this mechanism should be supported by clear, harmonised and easily accessible guidelines.

2.5. The EESC points out that the digitalisation and interoperability of authorisation, registration and monitoring procedures must be ensured, in order to simplify reporting obligations and reduce administrative costs, in particular for SMEs.

2.6. The EESC stresses that the proposal should include a periodic review mechanism, based on objective indicators, in order to assess the effectiveness of simplification and the reduction of administrative burdens, and to make any necessary adjustments.

International cooperation and competitiveness

3.1. The EESC underlines that international cooperation in the space sector is essential for the Union's competitiveness and strategic autonomy, and that the proposal must promote open and balanced international cooperation based on the principle of reciprocity.

3.2. The EESC considers that access to the Union's space market and services by third‑country entities should be governed by transparent and non‑discriminatory criteria, in compliance with the Union's international obligations and with the security and strategic interests of the Union and its Member States.

3.3. The EESC highlights the importance of promoting European technological and industrial leadership in the space sector, in particular in the areas of launch, Earth observation, satellite communications and navigation, and of ensuring that the regulatory framework supports the competitiveness of the European space industry on global markets.

3.4. The EESC stresses that the proposal must ensure consistency with the Union's international commitments, in particular those deriving from membership of the United Nations Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (UNCOPUOS), and must promote the dissemination of European standards and best practices at international level.

3.5. The EESC also considers it necessary to strengthen cooperation with partner countries and international organisations in the area of space traffic management and space debris mitigation, in order to ensure the long‑term sustainability of space activities.

3.6. The EESC stresses that the European space sector must be able to count on a stable and predictable regulatory framework, able to attract investment and foster innovation, and that the proposal must avoid any elements of uncertainty or excessive discretion.

Liability and insurance

4.1. The EESC considers that the proposal should include a clear and predictable regime of liability for damage caused by space activities, in line with the principles established by the Convention on International Liability for Damage Caused by Space Objects (the "Liability Convention") and with best practice at national and international level.

4.2. The EESC stresses that the obligation to take out insurance to cover potential damage should be proportionate to the risks associated with the activities and to the financial capacity of operators, with particular attention to SMEs and emerging industrial actors.

4.3. The EESC highlights that the proposal should provide for a clear mechanism for the settlement of disputes, both between operators and between operators and national or Union authorities, in order to ensure legal certainty and reduce litigation costs.
